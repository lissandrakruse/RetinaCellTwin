How the solver classifies evidence
  • Concordant: Welch FDR and Mann–Whitney FDR are both below 0.05.
  • Model-sensitive: only one corrected test is below 0.05.
  • Not supported at FDR 0.05: neither corrected test crosses the threshold.
  • Probability of direction: an approximate reference-posterior calculation from Hedges' g and its sampling variance, using a flat prior and Normal approximation. It is not the probability of retinal disease or causality.

Reference-posterior approximation

V(g) ≈ (nF + nG)/(nFnG) + g²/[2(nF + nG − 2)]

Pr(direction | data) ≈ Φ(|g|/√V(g)). The 95% interval is g ± 1.96√V(g).

Hypothesis decision

Not supported as a stable universal program: the RR-9 transcriptomic signature did not replicate in the independent OSD-758 microgravity-versus-ground contrast, and its attenuation under artificial gravity was not stronger than the transcriptome background.

A narrower, time-specific radiation-compatible component survived the benchmark and is retained for prospective testing. No causal or human-clinical claim is made.

Interpretation boundary

  • Spaceflight is a combined exposure and does not isolate microgravity.
  • Cross-dataset agreement is hypothesis-generating, not causal proof.
  • Mouse retina evidence does not establish human clinical risk.

Scientific foundation

Primary datasets, statistical methods and the closest prior retinal analysis are linked directly. The probabilistic layer is intentionally approximate and auditable.

  1. NASA OSDR Biological Data API — provenance and open-file access.
  2. Casaletto et al. (2026) — machine-learning analysis of molecular pathways of retinal damage in spaceflown mice; used as prior work, not duplicated as a new discovery.
  3. Kremsky et al. (2024) — prior demonstration that artificial gravity attenuates retinal and optic-nerve transcriptomic responses; the present audit tests transfer of an independently selected RR-9 panel.
  4. Kothiyal et al. (2022) — longitudinal retinal multi-omics after chronic low-dose irradiation and hindlimb unloading; experimental basis for OSD-203 attribution contrasts.
  5. Hedges (1981) — bias-corrected standardized mean-difference theory.
  6. Benjamini & Hochberg (1995) — false-discovery-rate control.
  7. Makowski et al. (2019) — probability of direction and Bayesian effect-existence indices.
  8. Strong et al. (2015) — expected value of sample information for research decisions.
  9. Corral-Acero et al. (2020) — digital-twin concepts and limitations in medicine.
