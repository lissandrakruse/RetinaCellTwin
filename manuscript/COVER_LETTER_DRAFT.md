Please consider our Research Article, “RetinaCellTwin: a reproducible multimodal and cross-mission audit of retinal responses to spaceflight, artificial gravity and ground analogues,” for the **Data-Driven Multicellular Systems Biology** thematic series.

RetinaCellTwin tests whether a 362-gene retinal spaceflight signature selected in NASA OSDR study OSD-255 transfers to the independent OSD-758 artificial-gravity experiment and whether remaining flight-associated effects can be bounded with time-resolved OSD-203 ground analogues. The principal result is a negative replication: the RR-9 signature does not behave as a stable, universal gravity signature. A narrower seven-day radiation-compatible component exceeds equal-sized random-panel benchmarks, but it is transient and is reported as hypothesis-generating rather than causal.

The manuscript also integrates public image-derived endpoints from OSD-557 and OSD-568 through a deliberately unpaired evidence matrix. Study-level contextual convergence is limited to the photoreceptor domain. Apoptosis is supported only by an imaging endpoint because no direct transcriptomic apoptosis test was prespecified, while oxidative-stress and vascular/barrier imaging domains do not pass the corrected threshold. We do not infer sample-level association, mediation, cell of origin, human SANS risk or individual-level prediction.

The work is relevant to the thematic series because it combines multicellular retinal context, high-throughput molecular and imaging data, reproducible computational analysis, open tools and a containerized workflow. The complete package includes input provenance and checksums, exact software environments, deterministic analyses, automated verification, frozen machine-readable results and publication figures. The current development package is available at <https://github.com/lissandrakruse/RetinaCellTwin>; the exact submitted version will be archived at **[insert v0.3.0 DOI after release]**.

The work's distinct contribution is not the rediscovery of previously reported RR-9 biology. It is the prespecified cross-mission transfer test, the transcriptome-background and random-panel controls, the independent effect-level audits, and the explicit reporting of where multimodal evidence does and does not converge.
